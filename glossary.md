# 📖 Viral Content Deep Analyst: Technical Glossary

### Attentional Channeling
The psychological practice of directing attention to a specific stimulus so the subconscious mind focuses exclusively on that concept and treats it as causal. (Robert Cialdini, *Pre-Suasion*).

### Brody Invariant
The empirical principle that real-time social engagement signals (Remixes, Shares, Comments) spike behind platform CDN edge caches minutes before the public view count refreshes, enabling early outlier detection before the video goes broad.

### Click, Whirr
The automatic, unthinking behavioral sequence triggered by an environmental cue. "Click" is the stimulus; "whirr" is the rollout of the automatic compliance tape. (Robert Cialdini, *Influence*).

### Curiosity Seed
A short provocative sentence or micro-loop inserted at the end of a section to compel the viewer to keep reading or watching without pausing. (Joseph Sugarman, *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*).

### Decoy / Trojan Caption
A misleading or automated caption written by creators (e.g. `comment "TEA" for the recipe #ad #health`) to satisfy affiliate ManyChat automation or evade algorithmic shadowbans while the actual video discusses sensitive medical topics.

### Dual-Process Theory
Psychological model separating cognitive processing into System 1 (fast, peripheral, emotional, heuristic-driven) and System 2 (slow, central, analytical). Short-form reels operate 95% within System 1.

### Fixed-Action Pattern
An innate, mechanical sequence of behaviors triggered by a specific stimulus (trigger feature).

### Life-Force 8 (LF8)
The eight fundamental human biological survival drives defined by Drew Eric Whitman: Survival, Food/Beverage, Freedom from Pain, Sexual Companionship, Comfort, Superiority, Care of Loved Ones, and Social Approval.

### ManyChat Funnel
An automated direct-response workflow where viewers are directed to comment a specific keyword on a video, automatically triggering an Instagram DM containing an affiliate link, PDF lead magnet, or digital product.

### Perceptual Contrast Principle
The psychological phenomenon where the perception of a second stimulus is significantly amplified when presented immediately after an opposing first stimulus (e.g., contrasting a painful $1,200 endoscopy against a $4 pantry tea).

### Privileged Moment
A brief window in time immediately preceding an appeal during which the recipient is particularly receptive to the message. (Robert Cialdini, *Pre-Suasion*).

### Psychological Reactance
The emotional response that occurs when a person feels their freedom to choose is being restricted or threatened, leading to an increased desire for the restricted item. (Scarcity).

### Single-Chute Framing
Formulating a question or opening premise such that the audience's mind can only travel down a single exploratory path that validates the hypothesis.

### Slippery Slide
The fundamental direct-response principle where every sentence and visual cut is engineered solely to compel the audience to consume the subsequent element without cognitive friction. (Joseph Sugarman).

### Zeigarnik Effect
The psychological tendency for people to remember uncompleted or interrupted tasks significantly better than completed ones, driving compulsive watch-through until the open loop is closed.
